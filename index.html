The design

How a fill runs six steps, coupling to coupling
  1. Connect the cascade whips to QC-01 oxygen, QC-02 helium and QC-03 air. Each cascade is a chain of cylinders on pigtails with a check valve per cylinder, so a low bottle cannot be back-filled by a full one.
  2. Connect the fill whip at QC-06 to the cylinder being filled, and open its valve.
  3. Open NV-04 DIRECT. Meter oxygen in with NV-01, watching PI-04, until the partial pressure you want is in the cylinder.
  4. Close NV-01. Meter helium in with NV-02, then air with NV-03 to top up. Each supply gauge PI-01 to PI-03 reads its own cascade, upstream of its valve.
  5. When the supply cannot push any further, close NV-04, open NV-05 TO BOOSTER and drive the booster from a scuba first stage at about 7 bar. Gas returns through QC-05 and the check valve CV-04 into the fill line.
  6. Close everything, open V-01 to bleed the fill line to the vent bulkhead, then part the couplings. The cascade chain bleeds do the same for the supply side.

Every valve in the gas path is a needle valve. Nothing opens fast enough to drive an adiabatic compression event in oxygen, which is the reason there is not a ball valve anywhere on the panel.

Decisions locked 2026-09-04, eleven of them
  1. Fill at 200 bar with PSV-01 set at 220 bar.
  2. Needle valves only in the gas path; a 3-way ball valve for the selector was assessed and declined, because it would move the adiabatic-compression safeguard out of the hardware and into an equalise-before-switching procedure.
  3. The selector stays two needle valves: NV-04 DIRECT and NV-05 TO BOOSTER.
  4. Quick couplings are FAV FDQRCO2 and are not keyed. Labels and colour bands identify the gas instead.
  5. No vent on disconnect. V-01 bleeds the fill line and the cascade chain bleeds vent the supply side before any coupling is parted.
  6. The cascade chain bleed sits just before the outlet whip; the single air pigtail carries its own bleed.
  7. Cascades are tee chains with short stainless hoses rather than a machined manifold bar.
  8. Needle valves rated 344 bar are accepted against the 300 bar maximum supply.
  9. Standard Swagelok or FAV parts cleaned in-house, with O-rings swapped to FKM. No factory oxygen variants.
  10. Master gauge is digital, PI-04.
  11. The case holds the panel only. The booster and the analyser travel in their own cases.

Before anything is bought

Open confirmations
  1. FAV FDQRCO2 couplings. Gas-tightness at 300 bar on helium, seal materials, poppet spring, locking mechanism, cycle life, and the rules for mating and parting. Six sit in the gas path and none of it is published. The fallback is DIN 477 handwheels and a change to the GA.
  2. In-house oxygen cleaning procedure. Written down before the first oxygen fill: disassemble, degrease, FKM and PTFE seals, Krytox, UV inspection. The schedule now depends on standard parts cleaned by hand, and an undocumented step is how a hydrocarbon lubricant survives in a valve.
  3. PSV-01 capacity and the booster stall curve. The relief must pass the XBD30-OL's flow, and the drive set point of about 7 bar is a ratio calculation, not vendor data.
  4. IS 3224 outlets on the actual cylinders. Legacy and imported valves are in circulation, and a wrong nut is a dead pigtail set.
  5. Case depth. Check the interior taper against the stack with a cardboard mock-up: the plate sits 30 mm below the rim with 112 mm clear underneath, and the walls narrow towards the floor.
  6. Hole sizes B, C, D and F. Nominal until the valves, couplings, bulkhead adaptor and vent union are in hand; drill pilot holes until then.
