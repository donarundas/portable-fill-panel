Portable Fill Station — cascade pigtail manifolds

The supply side of the fill panel: an Allegro-style high-pressure pigtail cascade kit redrawn for Indian industrial cylinders, which use IS 3224 threaded outlets rather than CGA fittings: a G 5/8 bullnose for oxygen, G 3/4 for helium, G 7/8 for air. Each cylinder gets the nut and nipple for its gas, a 0.6 m oxygen-clean pigtail and a check valve into a chain of ¼ in tees linked by short hoses; the chain ends in a bleed and the outlet whip with its labelled plug for the panel coupling. No manifold bar.

How the cascade is used

1Rig

Stand the cylinders, fit each cylinder nut and nipple by hand plus a quarter turn with the spanner, no washer, no grease, no tape. The O₂ set has G 5/8 bullnoses, the He set G 3/4 female nuts, the air set G 7/8, so a set only fits its own gas. The tee chain rests across the valve guards. Plug the outlet whip into the panel port with the matching colour band and label — the plugs are not keyed, so the label is the check.

2Cascade

Open one cylinder valve at a time, the lowest pressure first, and decant until it equalises with the fill cylinder. Shut it, open the next. The check valves CV-11..13 stop gas moving cylinder-to-cylinder if two valves are ever open together.

3Boost

When even the fullest cylinder is below the fill target, switch the panel to booster mode. The booster draws through the same bar and sweeps a supply cylinder down to about 30 bar, which is what makes helium affordable.

4Change a cylinder

Shut all cylinder valves, open the chain bleed V-11 next to the whip, wait for the panel supply gauge to read zero, then loosen the nut or part the plug. Never crack a bullnose nut under pressure. About 14 L of gas is lost per bleed at 150 bar — the price of the check valves.

Why it is drawn this way

  • IS 3224, not CGA — and gas-specific. Indian industrial cylinders use IS 3224:2002 outlets: No. 3 (G 5/8 RH female, bullnose) for oxygen, No. 20 (G 3/4 RH male) for helium, argon and nitrogen, No. 19 (G 7/8 RH) for air. Under BS 341 helium shares the oxygen No. 3 outlet, which is where the earlier "identical fittings" assumption came from; India differs. The cylinder end is therefore the one mechanical guard against cross-connection; the panel plugs are labelled and colour-banded, not keyed. [CONFIRM on the supplier's actual cylinders].
  • Tees, not a manifold bar. The first draft had a machined manifold bar. A chain of catalogue ¼ in tees linked by 0.4 m hoses does the same job: same check valves, same single bleed, same outlet whip, no custom block, no strap, less weight, and a fourth cylinder is one more tee. It is also what the Allegro reference kit does. Changed at review 2026-09-04.
  • Cylinder valves are the isolation. A needle valve in each leg would let the operator select without reaching the cylinder valve, at the cost of three valves per set. Not fitted; drawn as an option in note 7.
  • Check valves stay. Cheap, and they make the one-valve-at-a-time rule forgiving instead of critical.
  • One bleed per chain, next to the whip. With the cylinder valves shut, every pigtail and the whip vent through the check valves into the chain and out of the bleed, so no per-pigtail bleed is needed and the coupling needs no vent of its own. The single air pigtail gets its own bleed for the same reason.
  • No gauge on the chain. The panel's supply gauge reads it the moment the whip is plugged in.
